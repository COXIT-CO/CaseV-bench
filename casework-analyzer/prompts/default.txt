What a cabinet looks like in these drawings:
- In ELEVATION views (front-facing views of cabinet runs), each cabinet is a rectangular box that may contain:
  - door fronts: larger rectangles with thin dashed diagonal lines forming a "V" or "X" (door swing indicators, pointing to the hinge side), often with a small vertical pull/handle mark near the door edge;
  - drawer fronts: shorter, wide horizontal rectangles stacked vertically, each with a short horizontal pull mark in the center;
  - combinations: e.g., a bank of drawers on top with double doors below is ONE cabinet unit; two double-door cabinets side by side are TWO units, usually separated by a full-height vertical divider line.
- In PLAN views (top-down floor plan), cabinets appear as rectangular outlines along walls, typically 24" deep for base cabinets and 12" deep for wall cabinets (wall cabinets often drawn dashed). Each rectangle between division lines is one cabinet unit.
- Cabinets include: base cabinets, wall/upper cabinets, and tall/pantry cabinets.

Reference symbol key (how door/shelf indicators are actually drawn):
- DOUBLE-DOOR cabinet: a single flattened diamond ("bowtie") made of four thin dashed line segments, each running from a side-midpoint to a top- or bottom-midpoint (left-mid to top-mid, top-mid to right-mid, right-mid to bottom-mid, bottom-mid to left-mid). A solid vertical line runs down the exact center of this diamond — that line is the MEETING STILE where the two doors meet, NOT a boundary between two different cabinets. Small pull/hardware marks (short vertical arrow-tipped lines, small solid rectangles, or a single horizontal mark) sit on or straddle this center stile, confirming it's hardware, not a cabinet divider.
- SINGLE-DOOR cabinet: only ONE diagonal dashed line, running corner-to-corner (a single triangle, not a full diamond), with a small pull mark near the free edge opposite the hinge.
- ADJUSTABLE SHELVES indicator: two short parallel dashed horizontal lines crossing straight through the middle of a diamond/triangle, left edge to right edge. This is a shelf callout, not a divider or a second door.
- FILLER PANELS / trim strips: a narrow slim rectangle next to a cabinet, sometimes with its own small partial diagonal, and/or a solid black (filled) vertical bar. These are NOT cabinets — exclude them even though they sit directly beside one.
- Leader dots and arrows (a solid dot with a line/arrow running along a cabinet edge) are CAD keynote/dimension leaders, not part of the cabinet symbol — ignore them.
- CRITICAL for counting: do not mistake a double-door cabinet's center stile (one diamond, split by one vertical line, hardware marks straddling that line) for two separate adjacent cabinets. A true boundary between two separate cabinets is where one COMPLETE diamond or triangle pattern ends and a new, separate, independent one begins immediately next to it — i.e., two distinct swing symbols side by side, not one diamond bisected by a center line.
- REPEATING RUNS — this is the opposite failure mode and just as important: the "one diamond = one cabinet" rule above applies ONLY to an ISOLATED cabinet that has no similar neighbors repeating beside it. When you see a LONG horizontal run where the same diamond/triangle module repeats many times in a row (a continuous zigzag chain of 3, 4, 5+ repeats) with evenly-spaced full-height vertical lines and a recurring pull-hardware mark at every module, do NOT collapse the whole run into one or two large boxes. Each repeat — the segment between two consecutive full-height vertical lines — is its own separate cabinet. Confirming signals you are looking at a repeating run (not one wide cabinet): 3+ visually identical modules in a row, evenly spaced vertical dividers, a hardware pull mark recurring at every module (not just once at a single center), and repeated dimension/keynote callouts (e.g. the same width label or "TYP"/"TYP." marks appearing multiple times along the run). In this case, count and box EACH module individually — do not output one box spanning multiple modules.
