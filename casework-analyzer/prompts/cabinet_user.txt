WHAT A CABINET LOOKS LIKE
In elevation view, a cabinet is a rectangle (the door/drawer opening) containing
a symbol built from DASHED diagonal lines — never a solid crosshatch fill. The
dashed lines run from the box's corners, or from one shared point, toward the
midpoints of the opposite edges, forming one of these patterns:
  - A full diamond: four dashed lines meeting at all four edge midpoints
    (top, bottom, left, right).
  - A partial "bowtie": only the left and right tips are present (common when
    the box is wider than it is tall), so the shape looks like two triangles
    touching point-to-point at the horizontal centerline.
  - A "tent"/chevron: both dashed lines start from ONE shared point at the
    top-center of the box and fan down to the two bottom corners, instead of
    a symmetric diamond.
  - A single diagonal: just one dashed line running from one corner straight
    to the opposite corner, with no mirrored second line.
Look for these companion marks, which confirm it's a cabinet symbol rather
than random dashed geometry elsewhere on the sheet:
  - Arrowheads (">" "<" or doubled ">>" "<<") sitting exactly at the tip(s)
    where the dashed lines converge.
  - A short double-line segment ("=") on the centerline, between the two
    converging tips.
  - Small hardware marks near an edge or corner — a pair of short parallel
    vertical ticks, a single small rectangle, or a filled black dot. These
    are pulls/hinges and are separate from the diamond lines, not part of it.
  - On double-leaf units only: a SOLID (not dashed) vertical line splitting
    the box in half — this is the meeting stile between two doors. Solid vs.
    dashed is the key visual distinction between a stile and a swing line.
Single-leaf units (one door) show only the single-diagonal variant above, no
center divider, and one hardware mark near a corner. Cabinets usually appear
in a continuous run sitting on a counter or floor line, separated by these
solid stile lines from their neighbors.

WHAT IS NOT A CABINET
Do not count: the countertop surface itself, backsplash, open shelving
without doors, appliances, sinks, or trash/recycling grommet cutouts.
Do not count a plan-view rectangle as a cabinet unless it clearly shows
cabinet hatching/paneling.
